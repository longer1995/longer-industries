Manufacturing Process

  1. Blank Preparation: 6061-T6 sheet stock is blanked into circular discs (slug weight controlled ±0.5g)
  2. Cupping: Initial draw to form shallow cup (reduction ratio ~40%)
  3. Successive Draws: 3-4 progressive draws with intermediate anneals at 415°C to achieve final case length
  4. Head Forming: Primer pocket, flash hole, and headstamp formed by cold heading operation
  5. Mouth Trimming: Case mouth trimmed to length (46.0mm ±0.13mm), deburred
  6. T6 Heat Treatment: Solution heat treat at 530°C, water quench, age at 175°C/8hrs to restore T6 temper
  7. Type III Anodize: Hard anodize per MIL-A-8625 for corrosion protection and surface hardness
  8. Final Inspection: 100% go/no-go gauge for all critical dimensions, visual inspection for defects

Temperature Sensitivity

Propellant burn rate increases with temperature, directly affecting muzzle velocity and chamber pressure. The M2 charge is designed to remain within safe limits across the full operating range (−51°C to +63°C). At −51°C, expect velocity ~68 m/s (−10%); at +63°C, velocity ~82 m/s (+8%). All lot acceptance velocity testing is performed at 21°C ±3°C standard conditions. Temperature conditioning per MIL-STD-331 is required before proof testing at temperature extremes.

Shaped Charge Physics

Upon detonation of the Comp A5 fill, the detonation wave collapses the copper liner inward at ~2 km/s. The Munroe effect creates a hyper-velocity jet (tip ~6 km/s, tail ~2 km/s) and a slower-moving slug. The jet's kinetic energy density exceeds the yield strength of armor steel by orders of magnitude, penetrating via hydrodynamic flow — not melting. Penetration follows the Birkhoff formula: P/L = √(ρj/ρt) where P=penetration, L=jet length, ρj=jet density, ρt=target density. For copper jet vs. steel: P/L ≈ 0.94.

Critical manufacturing parameters: wall thickness uniformity determines jet coherence; asymmetry >0.03mm TIR causes jet breakup and reduces penetration 20–40%. Grain size affects ductility during collapse — too coarse and the liner fragments instead of forming a continuous jet. OFHC copper is specified because oxygen inclusions create porosity that disrupts jet formation.

Pressing Parameters

Comp A5 is pressed directly into the projectile body (in-situ pressing) in a single increment. The liner is pre-positioned in the body before pressing. Die and punch are hardened stainless steel, polished to <0.4 µm Ra. Pressing force is applied gradually (ramp rate ≤5,000 PSI/sec) to avoid adiabatic heating. Maximum pressing temperature must remain below 80°C — thermocouple monitoring on die is mandatory. Pressed pellet is weighed and density-verified (water immersion method) before proceeding.

Fragment Distribution Analysis

Arena fragmentation testing per MIL-STD-2105 demonstrates: ~300 primary fragments from the scored body, average mass 0.2g, distributed in a toroidal pattern concentrated ±30° from the equator. Fragment velocity at 5m is ~1,200 m/s (sufficient to penetrate 3mm mild steel). The scoring pattern is designed to produce fragments in the 0.1–0.4g range — heavy enough for lethality at range but light enough for high count. Fragments below 0.1g are considered secondary (from unscored areas) and contribute to casualty effect at close range.

Manufacturing & Heat Treatment

  1. Bar Stock: AISI 1045 per ASTM A108, cold-finished, certified chemistry
  2. Machining: CNC lathe — rough bore, rough OD, rough threads
  3. Scoring: Internal diamond-pattern scoring via EDM or broaching, 1.5mm depth (50% of wall)
  4. Normalizing: Heat to 845°C, hold 30 min, air cool to achieve HRC 20–28
  5. Finish Machining: Finish bore, threads, and OD to final dimensions
  6. Surface Treatment: Phosphate coating per MIL-DTL-16232 (corrosion protection)
  7. Inspection: 100% dimensional + hardness check; SPC on scoring depth

Advantage Over Steel Fragments

Tungsten's density (19.25 g/cm³) is 2.45× that of steel (7.85 g/cm³). For the same fragment mass, a tungsten sphere has smaller cross-section and thus higher sectional density — meaning it retains velocity better over distance. A 0.15g tungsten sphere at 1,200 m/s carries 108 J of kinetic energy and maintains lethal velocity (>60 J threshold per NATO STANAG 2920) to ~15m, compared to ~10m for equivalent steel fragments. The spherical shape also produces a predictable drag coefficient (Cd ≈ 0.47) for consistent ballistic modeling.

Impact Break-Away

The ogive must fracture cleanly on impact to expose the fuze to the target surface. The break-away zone is engineered via a stress-concentration groove at the ogive-body junction. The force must be low enough to ensure function at oblique angles (up to 70°) and on soft targets (sand, soil), but high enough to survive launch forces (~5,000g setback for ~10ms). The glass-fiber orientation from injection molding is controlled to create the desired failure mode — circumferential fibers at the break zone, axial fibers in the main body for hoop strength during flight.

Arming Sequence

  1. In Storage: Rotor is locked out-of-line (OOL) by setback pin and centrifugal detents. Detonator is not aligned with firing pin or booster. The round is bore-safe.
  2. Launch (Setback): ~5,000g acceleration drives setback pin rearward, releasing first safety interlock
  3. In-Bore Spin-Up: Rifling engages rotating band, spinning projectile to ~3,700 RPM
  4. Post-Muzzle (14–28m): Centrifugal force on detents overcomes spring, releasing rotor. Rotor aligns detonator in-line with firing pin and booster. Fuze is now ARMED.
  5. Impact (SQ): Nose contact drives firing pin through creep spring into detonator → detonator → booster → main charge
  6. Impact (Graze): At oblique angles, inertial element shears graze pin, driving detonator into firing pin via alternative path → same explosive train

Function & Requirements

The rotating band serves three critical functions: (1) Spin stabilization — engages rifling to impart gyroscopic stability for accurate flight; (2) Obturation — deforms into rifling grooves to seal propellant gases behind the projectile; (3) Centering — keeps the projectile axially aligned in the bore. Pure copper is used because it is soft enough to engrave cleanly into rifling without damaging the barrel (HV 40–60) yet strong enough to transmit the torque needed for spin-up. The band must be free of voids, cracks, and inclusions that could cause gas blow-by or asymmetric engraving.

Statistical Process Control (SPC)

X-bar and R Chart Setup

For each critical dimension, maintain X-bar (mean) and R (range) charts with subgroup size n=5. Sample every 50th unit (or per shift minimum). Calculate control limits from initial 25 subgroups:

UCL_Xbar = X̄ + A₂ × R̄   (A₂ = 0.577 for n=5)
LCL_Xbar = X̄ − A₂ × R̄
UCL_R = D₄ × R̄       (D₄ = 2.114 for n=5)
LCL_R = D₃ × R̄       (D₃ = 0 for n=5)

Gauge Repeatability & Reproducibility

All measurement systems for critical dimensions must pass GR&R study per AIAG MSA 4th Edition. Three operators, 10 parts, 3 trials each = 90 measurements per study.

%GR&R ResultDecisionAction
<10%AcceptableMeasurement system approved
10–30%MarginalMay be acceptable — review with engineering
>30%UnacceptableMust improve measurement system before use

Number of distinct categories (ndc) must be ≥5. If ndc <5, the gauge cannot reliably distinguish between parts — resolution is insufficient.

CAPA (Corrective and Preventive Action)

  1. Problem Definition: 5-Why root cause analysis (mandatory for critical/safety NCRs)
  2. Containment: Immediate action to prevent further defective output
  3. Root Cause: Fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram — Man, Machine, Material, Method, Measurement, Environment
  4. Corrective Action: Eliminate root cause, update procedures/work instructions
  5. Preventive Action: Systemic improvements (error-proofing, poka-yoke, design change)
  6. Verification: Confirm actions effective — measure results over 90-day monitoring period
  7. Closure: QA Manager sign-off after verification period

Explosives Safety Regulatory Framework

DDESB Requirements (DoD 6055.09-M)

  • Explosives Safety Site Plan: Required before any explosive operations commence. Must show all buildings, inhabited building distances (IBD), public traffic route distances (PTR), inter-magazine distances (IMD)
  • Quantity-Distance (Q-D): Minimum separation distances based on Net Explosive Weight (NEW) per building
  • Maximum Credible Event (MCE): Worst-case explosive event analysis for each operational building
  • Personnel Limits: Maximum number of personnel allowed in each explosive area based on MCE and mission necessity
  • Construction Standards: Barricaded operations for pressing, earth-covered magazines for storage per DoD 6055.09-M
  • Lightning Protection: Per MIL-STD-1576 and NFPA 780 for all explosive storage and operations areas
  • Waivers/Exemptions: DDESB-approved for any Q-D deficiency (rare, documented justification required)

Emergency Response Procedures

▶

🔥 Fire in Production Area

CRITICAL
  1. EVACUATE IMMEDIATELY — Do not attempt to fight an explosive fire
  2. Sound fire alarm (pull station at each exit)
  3. Evacuate to designated assembly point (minimum 300m from explosive buildings)
  4. Account for all personnel (buddy system roster)
  5. Call 911, then notify on-site Safety Officer
  6. Brief responding fire department on explosive quantities and types present
  7. Do NOT re-enter until "All Clear" from Safety Officer and fire department
  8. If fire involves Class 1.1D — establish 600m evacuation radius
▶

💥 Accidental Detonation

CRITICAL
  1. Take cover immediately if still in building
  2. After initial event, evacuate to 600m assembly point
  3. Render first aid to injured — do not move patients with suspected spinal injury
  4. Call 911 — report explosion, estimated casualties, explosive type
  5. Notify Safety Officer, Plant Manager, DCMA QAR
  6. Preserve scene for investigation (do not clean up or move anything)
  7. Notify DDESB within 24 hours per DoD 6055.09-M
  8. Initiate Accident Investigation Board (AIB) per local procedures
▶

🧪 Chemical Spill (Propellant/Solvent)

CAUTION
  1. Alert personnel in immediate area
  2. If propellant spill: eliminate all ignition sources (shut down equipment)
  3. Don appropriate PPE (nitrile gloves, face shield, respirator if vapor present)
  4. Contain spill using absorbent material (vermiculite for propellant, spill pillows for solvents)
  5. Do NOT sweep dry propellant — wet down first with water spray
  6. Collect contaminated material in proper waste container
  7. Notify Safety Officer and Environmental Manager
  8. Document spill (quantity, time, location, response) per SPCC plan
▶

🏥 Medical Emergency

CAUTION
  1. Call 911 / on-site medical
  2. Begin first aid (CPR, bleeding control) — all production personnel trained in Stop-the-Bleed
  3. If in explosive area: move patient only if area is safe; otherwise treat in place
  4. AED locations: every production building entrance (checked monthly)
  5. Trauma kit locations: every workstation (checked weekly)
  6. Guide EMS to location — provide chemical exposure information from SDS binder

Environmental Controls

Temperature

All explosive operations: 15–30°C (59–86°F). Continuous monitoring with alarmed data loggers. HVAC dedicated to explosive areas — no recirculated air.

Humidity

Propellant/explosive areas: 30–65% RH. Dehumidification required in humid climates. Propellant absorbs moisture → affects burn rate.

Static Control

All explosive areas: conductive flooring, grounding straps, ionizers. Personnel ground before entering. Max surface resistance: 10⁶ Ω. ESD audits quarterly.

Lightning

All explosive buildings: lightning protection per NFPA 780 + MIL-STD-1576. Operations suspended when lightning within 5 NM. Storm detection system required.
